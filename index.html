About me

Creative first, code-literate

I'm Chiara Melagrani, a Computer Science student at Lamar University in Beaumont, Texas, specializing in Computer Game Development. I graduate in December 2026. My coursework has given me a foundation in programming with Java, Python, C++, and C#, along with game engines like Unity, software design, problem solving, and team-based development.

My main technical interest is the creative side of games: character design, storytelling, and the systems that make players care about a world. I like the point where art and code meet, which is why I build tools like OC Forge, an AI assistant that turns a few seed ideas into a complete character sheet with a backstory, personality, color palette, and design notes.

Outside the classroom, I run my own business as a Roblox UGC creator. I design and sell dynamic heads, animated faces that give avatars real expressions, both as public Marketplace releases and private one-of-one commissions. My most popular head has more than 271,000 favorites, and the business earns about $200,000 a year. That work taught me to study what players want, ship on a schedule, and improve designs from real feedback. I also take character art commissions and work as a shift supervisor at Colorado Canyon, where I lead and train a team.

My career goal is to join a game studio in a narrative design, character design, or game design role, ideally remote, where I can help create memorable characters and stories. Over the next few years, I want to deepen my skills in game engines and technical art, contribute to a shipped title, and grow toward leading the creative direction of characters and worlds.

Things I've built

Project 1 · AI assistant

OC Forge: AI Character Builder

OC Forge turns a few seed ideas, like a name, archetype, setting, and traits, into a complete original character sheet: backstory, personality, likes and dislikes, voice lines, a color palette, design notes, and story hooks. It sends a structured prompt to the AI model the user chooses, checks the reply against a schema, and renders it safely. A no-key demo mode lets anyone try it, and sheets can be exported as Markdown or JSON.

Project 2 · Course project · Team of 3

Procedural Maze Generator

A 3D maze game built in Unity for CPSC 4370 Artificial Intelligence (Fall 2025). Each time the game starts, a randomized depth-first search carves a new 21×21 maze that is always solvable. The player moves a third-person character through it while an AI agent uses A* pathfinding to walk the shortest route to the exit as a guide. Built with Issac Gros and Kody Braun.
